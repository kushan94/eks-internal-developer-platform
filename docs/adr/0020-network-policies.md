# ADR 0020: Isolate every service's inbound traffic with NetworkPolicies

**Status:** Accepted (2026-11-13)

## Context
Any pod could reach any other pod in the cluster. A compromised or misbehaving service in one team's namespace could call another team's services and connect to their in-cluster databases. Production platforms limit that by default. On EKS, the VPC CNI also ignores NetworkPolicies unless enforcement is turned on, so policies alone would give a false sense of safety.

## Decision
- **The composition gives every Kubernetes `WebService` a NetworkPolicy** that only lets these reach its pods:
  - pods in its own namespace
  - Prometheus in `monitoring`
  - for `public: true`, the load balancer, on the service's port. The ALB sends traffic straight to pod IPs from the public subnets, whose ranges reach the composition through the `aws-platform` EnvironmentConfig as `aws.publicSubnetCidrs`.
- **In-cluster databases get their own policy:** their namespace, the CloudNativePG operator in `cnpg-system`, and Prometheus.
- **The VPC CNI add-on enforces policies** (`enableNetworkPolicy`). kind's default network plugin enforces them too.
- **Only inbound traffic is restricted.** Outbound stays open. ([ADR 0024](0024-egress-policies.md) restricts it.)
- **Every pod in a team namespace is covered.** Teams can only create `WebService`s there, so every pod comes from the composition.
- **Testing:**
  - `make test` checks the rendered policies' shape, including the load balancer rule.
  - On a throwaway kind cluster, the exact rendered policy let traffic in from the service's namespace and `monitoring`, and blocked it from another team's namespace, which could reach the pod before the policy was applied.

## Alternatives considered
- **A default-deny policy per namespace, generated by Kyverno:** also covers pods that don't come from a `WebService`, but adds generate rules to maintain. Per-service policies come from the same template as everything else a service gets.
- **Restricting outbound traffic too:** stronger. But every service then needs explicit allowances for DNS, RDS and any external API it calls, and database pods also need the API server, whose addresses EKS manages. That's the next step, ideally with an API field for what a service may call.
- **Security groups for pods:** AWS-native, but EKS-only, and per pod rather than per label.

## Consequences
- **Services in different namespaces can't call each other unless allowed.** `spec.allowFrom` lists the teams, or a team's single service, that may call a service on its port.
  - Callers are identified by the `platform.example.org/team` label Argo CD puts on team namespaces, which teams can't set, and by the name label the composition puts on pods. A team can't pass itself off as another.
  - With environments, callers must also be in the same environment, so dev can't call prod.
  - On a throwaway kind cluster, the rendered policy allowed the listed team, and the listed team's one service, from prod. It blocked the same callers from dev, another service of the allowed team, and a pod with the allowed name in another team's namespace.
- **The policies only apply to the Kubernetes runtime.** ECS services rely on their security groups, which already only allow the VPC.
